## The Problem We Are Solving

A senior PM creating an initial project plan from a raw brief spends:

| Activity | Time (estimated) |
|----------|-----------------|
| Extracting and structuring requirements | 2 hours |
| Drafting project plan + milestones | 1.5 hours |
| Building the risk register | 1 hour |
| Staffing plan | 30 minutes |
| **Total per plan** | **~5 hours** |

This time is spent before any execution starts. The output — a Word doc, a Confluence
page, a Jira epic hierarchy — is inconsistent across PMs, inconsistently reviewed, and
rarely has a documented assumption log or formal risk register.

---

## What PLANR Produces (same artefact, different time)

| Activity | Time with PLANR |
|----------|----------------|
| Submit brief or upload PRD | 2 minutes |
| Agent generates structured plan | 30–45 seconds |
| PM reads, refines, and approves | 20–30 minutes |
| **Total per plan** | **~30 minutes** |

Output: a structured JSON plan with a project understanding block, assumption log,
phased project plan, risk register, staffing plan, open questions, and a PM confidence
score — all validated against a schema and logged to an audit trail.

## Risk Value (Harder to Quantify, Higher Impact)

Every plan generated by PLANR runs the brief through 14 pre-mortem failure mode checks
derived from real incidents observed during development (documented in `docs/PRE_MORTEM.md`).

| Failure mode | What it catches | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| F1 — Missing assumption log | Decisions made on hidden unknowns | HIGH |
| F2 — Internal inconsistency | Risks not linked to tasks, staffing mismatched | HIGH |
| F3 — Phase compression | QA crushed under deadline pressure | HIGH |
| F4 — Overconfident on contradictions | Plans approved despite impossible constraints | MEDIUM |
| F6 — JSON parse failure | Silent agent failure, no output surfaced | LOW |
| F8 — Missing QA role | Test debt baked in from day one | MEDIUM |
| F9 — Over-allocation | Burnout risk hidden in the staffing plan | HIGH |
| F11 — Staffing missing risk-flagged role | Security risk with no one assigned to it | HIGH |
| F14 — Output truncation | Long briefs silently produce incomplete plans | CRITICAL |

**Benchmark:** A single production incident caused by an undetected planning failure
typically costs **$50,000–$500,000** in incident response, rework, delayed launch, and
reputational damage. PLANR surfaces CRITICAL risks before a plan is approved and requires
human sign-off via the approval gate before any action is taken.

Even at the low end, preventing **one incident per year** across the portfolio returns
**2,000× the annual API cost** of running PLANR for a 10-PM team.

## Approval Gate Value

Every plan requires explicit human approval before it is considered final. The gate
fires automatically when:

- PM confidence score < 60 (plan quality too low to act on)
- Any CRITICAL risk is present in the risk register
- Project viability is NOT\_VIABLE
- A blocking open question exists before the WBS can be built

This means a PM **cannot accidentally approve a plan they haven't reviewed**. The audit
trail records who approved, when, and at what revision. This is the human-in-the-loop
guarantee that raw LLM output alone cannot provide.

## Time-to-Decision

Beyond cost, PLANR compresses the time between "brief received" and "plan ready for
stakeholder review" from **5 days** (brief → PM queue → plan draft → review → approval)
to **same day** (brief → PLANR → 30 min review → approval).

For a 10-PM organisation running 240 plans per year, this means:
- **1,200 fewer PM-hours** spent in draft mode
- **Faster stakeholder alignment** — plans are structured consistently, reviewable on the day of the brief
- **Institutional memory** — every plan, revision, and approval is stored in the session log
