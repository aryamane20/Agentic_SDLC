## STEP 7: RISK IDENTIFICATION

### Mandatory Risk Checklist — evaluate every item regardless of input:

□ Is deadline fixed AND scope is still undefined? → Schedule Risk, probability HIGH, impact MEDIUM = HIGH
  (Not CRITICAL. Scope definition is normal PM work resolved in discovery.)
□ Does the team have direct experience with this technology stack? → Technical Risk
  If not explicitly stated → UNKNOWN → Execution Risk, MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact = MEDIUM
□ Are there ≥ 2 external system dependencies?
  Count every named external system AND every external service
  (email provider, notification service, auth provider) as 1 each.
  If total ≥ 2 → Integration Risk, MEDIUM probability.
□ Is team size < 3 for a project longer than 2 months? → Resource Risk
  If team_size ≥ 5 → skip. No resource risk on a full team.
□ Has the stakeholder formally approved scope in writing?
  → Scope Risk, MEDIUM probability, HIGH impact = HIGH score.
  (Absence of written sign-off is MEDIUM probability, not HIGH — it is resolved in week 1.
  But if scope disputes materialise, impact is HIGH. Score = HIGH, not CRITICAL.)
□ Are there compliance, security, or regulatory requirements? → Compliance Risk
  If none stated and the project is an internal tool → skip. Do not invent compliance risks.
□ Is this the team's first project of this type?
  If not explicitly stated → UNKNOWN → Execution Risk, MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact = MEDIUM
□ Are any critical path roles single-person with no backup?
  If team_size ≤ 3 OR any role part-time → Continuity Risk, MEDIUM probability, HIGH impact = HIGH
  If team_size > 3 AND all roles full-time → skip entirely.

### MANDATORY: LEGACY / DEPRECATED / UNSUPPORTED API RISKS

If the input names any of the following, add at least one Integration or Technical risk
with score = "CRITICAL" (HIGH probability + HIGH impact):
- Direct database access to a legacy system (no supported REST/gRPC API)
- Deprecated, undocumented, or vendor-flagged EOL APIs the build depends on
- Any integration the brief calls out as unsupported, sunset, or off-the-supported-path

Name the specific system in description and trigger. This rule overrides generic HIGH calibration.
This is the ONLY scenario where CRITICAL is allowed when BRIEF QUALITY CAP is active.

### Risk fields (per risk):
- id: "R1", "R2", ... (string, not "R-1")
- category: "Schedule" | "Resource" | "Technical" | "Scope" | "Integration" | "Compliance" | "Budget"
- description: "If X happens, then Y will occur" format
- probability: "HIGH" | "MEDIUM" | "LOW"
- impact: "HIGH" | "MEDIUM" | "LOW"
- score: derived from matrix below
- trigger: observable event signaling this risk is materializing
- mitigation: specific action to reduce probability BEFORE it happens
- contingency: specific action to take IF it happens anyway

### Risk scoring matrix:
```
               HIGH Impact   MED Impact   LOW Impact
HIGH Prob   →  CRITICAL      HIGH         MEDIUM
MED Prob    →  HIGH          MEDIUM       LOW
LOW Prob    →  MEDIUM        LOW          LOW
```

### Probability anchors:
HIGH: risk will materialize if no action taken AND an observable trigger is already described
  in the input (not merely imaginable). Examples: deadline fixed + scope still undefined;
  compliance obligation with no plan; input explicitly states part-time staff, shared
  resources, or competing projects.
MEDIUM: risk may materialize depending on execution — external API dependency, new technology,
  no backup for key role, first project of this type, vague success criteria,
  missing written commitment when the brief otherwise states a concrete team and plan.
LOW: risk is unlikely given current signals — experienced team, known stack, small
  well-defined scope, adequate team size, input does not warn of overload.

### Hard calibration — do not manufacture HIGH probability:

- **When in doubt: default to MEDIUM, not HIGH.** HIGH probability is rare on well-specified briefs.
- **Only use HIGH probability when the trigger condition is explicitly present in the input text.**
  Before writing HIGH, identify the specific phrase from the input that justifies it.
  If you cannot identify a phrase, the probability is MEDIUM, not HIGH.
- **Absence of a written sign-off is NOT a HIGH-probability trigger.** Log it as Scope Risk
  with MEDIUM probability and HIGH impact (score HIGH, not CRITICAL).
- **Resource / availability (well-specified briefs):** If the brief names team composition
  (counts and roles), stack experience, and timeline, and does NOT warn of contention,
  part-time work, or multi-project pull — use MEDIUM probability for
  "developers might be diverted" / "capacity unconfirmed in writing" class risks.
  Reserve HIGH probability for active signals in the brief: competing priorities,
  fractional FTE, matrixed shared services, or explicit delivery risk language.
- **Schedule / timeline risks:** Do NOT score a schedule compression risk as HIGH probability
  when the deadline matches a defensible duration for the project type and team size
  (~12–16 weeks for TYPE_A internal web app with ~3 experienced devs on a known stack is
  within normal range). Omit a standalone "timeline is aggressive" risk in that case, or
  if you must keep one for audit: MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact at most (score MEDIUM).
  Never produce HIGH/HIGH → CRITICAL from generic schedule worry on a proportionate plan.

### CRITICAL score — definition and hard limits:

CRITICAL means: without immediate escalation, the project CANNOT be executed at all.
Reserved exclusively for: deprecated/EOL/unsupported APIs (see MANDATORY rule above),
missing core infrastructure with no workaround, or show-stopping compliance blockers.

NEVER CRITICAL (these are standard PM work, not project killers):
- Undefined success metrics, KPIs, or dashboard content → pre-sprint BA/analyst work → max HIGH
- Absence of written scope approval → resolved in week-1 sign-off meeting → max HIGH
- Technology stack not yet selected → MEDIUM probability (decision pending)
- Single-person role on a 5-person full-time team → MEDIUM (manageable with pairing)
- Data availability or schema gaps in existing supported systems → discovery sprint → max HIGH
- Any risk a PM workshop, discovery session, or stakeholder meeting in week 1 resolves

### Concrete calibration examples:

"Dashboard metrics may not be pre-defined in Snowflake / BigQuery / Redshift."
→ Probability: MEDIUM (data analyst audits schema in week 1).
   Impact: HIGH (if completely missing, scope must shrink).
   Score: HIGH. Not CRITICAL.

"No formal written scope sign-off from stakeholders."
→ Probability: MEDIUM. Impact: HIGH. Score: HIGH.
   (This is the correct treatment — week-1 PM action, but impact is real.)

"Snowflake query performance may not meet dashboard SLA."
→ Probability: MEDIUM. Impact: MEDIUM. Score: MEDIUM.

Minimum: never fewer than 3 risks. Every project has at least 3.
Sort final register: CRITICAL first, then HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW.

### Use Case Risk Mapping:
For each risk, identify which use case(s) from use_case_model are most affected.
Build risk_use_case_mapping: { "R1": ["UC2"], "R3": ["UC1", "UC3"] }.
Also identify which task IDs (from project_plan) are at high risk and flag as

     If any CRITICAL exists, downgrade to HIGH.

2. For each HIGH risk: confirm there is an explicit trigger present in the input.
   If no trigger text exists → downgrade to MEDIUM.

3. Confirm sort order: CRITICAL → HIGH → MEDIUM → LOW.
